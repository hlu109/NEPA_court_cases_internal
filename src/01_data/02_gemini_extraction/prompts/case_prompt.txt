### **General Instructions**
- You are given one or two files containing the complete text of an **opinion from a U.S. appellate circuit court**: an HTML version, a PDF version, or both. If both are provided, you may cross-reference them (the PDF often preserves the original judge panel/byline formatting more reliably than the HTML rendering).
- Each case is litigation related to the National Environmental Policy Act.
- Because this court case is at the appellate level, the case has already gone through a district court, and either the plaintiff or defendant appealed the district ruling.
- The defendant in the district case can usually be assumed to be a federal agency or department. The plaintiff in the district case can usually be assumed to be a party challenging the government agency/department's execution of NEPA requirements.
- Use knowledge of the American legal system to inform your reasoning.
- **All letters are in the English alphabet and all words and names are in the English language.**
- **Do not make up any false information.**

### **Extraction Rules**

**disposition**: The final ruling ordered by the judges presiding over the appealed case. They can either affirm the district court, reverse the district court, or rule in a more complicated, "mixed" way.

**district_outcome**: How the district court originally ruled before the case was appealed. The district court could have ruled in favor of the plaintiff, defendant, or in a more complex, "mixed" way. 


**panel_judges**: A list of the names of **all appellate (circuit court) judges** who presided over this case. Include all judges on the panel: the judge who wrote the majority opinion, any judges who wrote concurrences or dissents, and any judges who joined without writing separately. If the opinion was en banc, return a list with a single entry saying "EN BANC". Judges are typically identified in one or more of the following locations near the top of the opinion:
    - A "Before:" line listing the full panel, e.g. "Before: WILKINS and KATSAS, Circuit Judges, and ROGERS, Senior Circuit Judge."
    - An author byline, e.g. "KATSAS, Circuit Judge:" or "SENTELLE, Chief Judge:"
    - An attribution line, e.g. "Opinion for the Court filed by Circuit Judge HENDERSON." or "Judge DUNCAN wrote the opinion, in which Judge MOTZ and Judge KING joined."

**opinion_authors**: A list of the names of all judges who **wrote** the opinion. If the opinion is per curiam (written collectively, no single named author), return a list with a single entry saying "PER CURIAM". If the opinion was written en banc, return a list with a single entry saying "EN BANC". Authors are typically identified by:
    - An author byline at the start of the opinion, e.g. "KATSAS, Circuit Judge:" or "SENTELLE, Chief Judge:"
    - An attribution line, e.g. "Opinion for the Court filed by Circuit Judge HENDERSON." or "Judge DUNCAN wrote the opinion..."

#### Name rules (apply to panel_judges and opinion_authors)
- **Do NOT include** judges that did not participate in the final decision-making in the case. (For instance, if an appellate judge was elevated to the federal Supreme Court, or was otherwise removed from the panel midway through the case, they should not be included.)
- **Do NOT include district court judges**. District judges are often identifiable by the title "District Judge" or "U.S.D.J.". The only **exception** is if a district judge is sitting by designation on the appellate panel, so long as the district judge actually participates in deciding the appeal alongside the circuit judges (i.e., because Federal law allows district court judges to temporarily fill a seat on an appellate panel when the circuit is short-staffed).
- **Do NOT include** attorneys, agency officials, or any other proper names that are not appellate judges.
- If the **full name** of a judge is given (e.g. "Karen LeCraft Henderson", "Harry T. Edwards"), use the full name.
- If only a **last name** is given (e.g. "KATSAS", "ROGERS"), use just the last name.
- Do not include judicial titles (e.g. "Circuit Judge", "Senior Circuit Judge", "Chief Judge") in the output names.
- If you cannot identify any judges from the text, return an empty list for that field.
- Order the names as they appear in the opinion.


### **Important Constraints**

- If you cannot figure out an attribute from the given text, you may enter "UNK".
- Extract only from the opinion itself – do not infer judge names from case citations or quoted prior opinions.
- Use knowledge of the American legal system to inform your reasoning.
